👀 See Like a Contractor

The call: “Can you fix my fence? It should be quick.”

The customer sees one leaning gate. A contractor sees a connected system.

Structure

Posts, rails, boards, gate frame, fasteners, movement, rot, and previous repairs.

Site

Grade, drainage, vegetation, access, neighboring property, and work area.

Boundary

Property line, ownership, shared fence questions, and survey uncertainty.

Utilities

Excavation, public locates, private lines, irrigation, and exterior lighting.

Customer Goal

Temporary function, safe repair, appearance match, full replacement, or resale preparation.

Money & Risk

Visible work, hidden conditions, exclusions, change orders, and cost of return trips.

Professional habit: ask what system created the failure—not only what part looks broken.

🧠 Think Like a Contractor

Verify before promising

Price, schedule, permit status, material availability, access, and repairability depend on facts.

Sequence before speed

Correct order prevents rework. Discovery, protection, demolition, inspection, curing, and finish work create dependencies.

Protect the whole project

Every decision affects safety, quality, schedule, cash flow, property, and customer trust.

Document the decision

Photos, notes, scope, estimate, selections, changes, and approvals form the job memory.

Beginner trap: fast answers feel professional. Verified answers are professional.

📏 Measuring & Site Notes

Measure the right property

Length, width, height, thickness, diameter, area, volume, slope, square, level, and plumb answer different questions.

Sketch the location

A number without a location becomes useless. Use labels, arrows, photo references, and notes.

Check variation

Existing conditions are rarely perfectly square, level, plumb, flat, or consistent.

Verify before ordering

Recheck custom, expensive, long-lead, or difficult-to-return products.

📋 Scope of Work

“Fix the fence” is not a scope. It is a request for discovery.

Existing Condition

What is present, visibly damaged, and still unknown?

Included Work

Demolition, preparation, material, installation, cleanup, disposal, and closeout.

Excluded Work

Hidden rot, utility relocation, survey work, painting, or customer-supplied product risk.

Change Process

Who approves added, removed, or changed work—and how price and time change.

💰 Estimating & Profit

Direct Cost

Labor, material, equipment, subcontractors, delivery, travel, and disposal.

Overhead

Insurance, bond, licensing, office, vehicle, tools, software, marketing, and administration.

Contingency

Defined allowance for uncertainty—not permission to skip discovery.

Profit

Return for ownership risk, capital, systems, and judgment after costs.

Cash Flow

A profitable job can still fail if purchases and payroll happen before customer money arrives.

Scope Risk

Unclear work creates unpriced labor, material, and return visits.

⚠️ Safety & Risk

People

Workers, clients, children, tenants, neighbors, and the public may be exposed to the work.

Property

Protect floors, walls, vehicles, landscaping, utilities, adjacent property, and occupied areas.

Task Hazards

Falls, electricity, excavation, dust, lead, silica, chemicals, lifting, tools, traffic, and weather.

Stop-Work Judgment

Pause when the condition is outside the scope, license, safe method, or verified information.

🏡 Fence Challenge

A homeowner requests a “quick gate adjustment.” The gate drags, the latch misses, one post moves by hand, and the grade slopes toward the post.

Inspect

Post movement, concrete, rot, hinges, fasteners, gate frame, opening square, grade, drainage, fence alignment, and access.

Ask

How long has it moved? Was drainage changed? Is temporary repair acceptable? Who owns the fence?

Measure

Opening at several heights, diagonals, post plumb, grade difference, damaged length, and access width.

Do Not Promise Yet

Final price, duration, boundary accuracy, material match, or hidden-condition cost.
