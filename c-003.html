🗣️ Construction Language

Shared Meaning

Terms allow owners, contractors, designers, inspectors, suppliers, and trades to describe the same thing.

Context

A term can change by trade or drawing context. Read notes, details, specifications, and manufacturer instructions.

Verification

Never hide confusion. Ask what the term means in this project.

Field Use

Language becomes valuable when it guides measurement, product selection, sequence, or inspection.

🗺️ Plan View

Viewpoint

A horizontal view looking down from above.

What It Shows

Walls, rooms, openings, fixtures, dimensions, and layout.

What It Hides

Vertical relationships and hidden layers may require elevations, sections, or details.

Field Check

Compare plan dimensions with actual conditions.

🏢 Elevation

Viewpoint

A vertical face viewed straight on.

What It Shows

Heights, openings, finishes, alignment, and exterior or interior appearance.

Use

Useful for siding, cabinets, tile, trim, and facade layout.

Question

Which elevation controls when field conditions differ?

✂️ Section

Cut View

Shows what would be visible if the assembly were cut through.

Layers

Framing, insulation, sheathing, finishes, flashing, and connections.

Sequence

Helps reveal what must be installed before another layer.

Risk

Ignoring a section can miss hidden structural or water-management requirements.

🔍 Detail

Purpose

Enlarges a difficult connection.

Examples

Ledger attachment, window flashing, footing, stair connection, or waterproofing transition.

Cross-Reference

Details are often referenced from plan or section symbols.

Field Use

Use details during layout, installation, and inspection.

📑 Specifications

Products

Manufacturer, type, grade, performance, color, or approved equal.

Installation

Preparation, fastening, curing, testing, and protection.

Quality

Finish level, tolerances, mockups, and acceptance criteria.

Conflict

Request clarification when plan, specification, and field condition disagree.

🚪 Schedules

Purpose

Organize repeated items.

Examples

Door, window, finish, hardware, equipment, and room schedules.

Cross-Check

Match schedule marks with plan symbols.

Procurement

Schedules can drive product ordering and lead-time review.

📐 Dimensions & Scale

Written Dimensions

Use written dimensions instead of estimating from appearance.

Scale

Scale helps interpret a drawing but does not replace critical written dimensions.

Units

Confirm feet, inches, decimals, metric, and elevation reference.

Revision

Verify the drawing revision before using dimensions.

🏚️ Field Verification

Plans

Show intended conditions.

Field

Shows actual conditions.

Measure

Verify custom, expensive, structural, and difficult-to-return work.

Document

Photo and record conflicts before fabrication or installation.

🎮 Conflicting Opening Challenge

Drawing

Graphic appears to show 36 inches.

Written Dimension

34 inches.

Field Framing

33½ inches.

Correct Move

Stop ordering, check details and schedule, document field condition, and request clarification.
