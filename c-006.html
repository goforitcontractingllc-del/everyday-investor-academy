🔨 Hammer Systems

Claw Hammer

General driving and pulling. Smooth faces reduce marking; milled faces grip but can damage finished surfaces.

Framing Hammer

Longer handle and heavier head for framing. More force also means more control is required.

Finish Hammer

Lighter and smoother for trim and delicate work.

Dead-Blow / Mallet

Moves or seats material with reduced rebound or surface damage.

📏 Measuring Tools

Tape Measure

Hook movement, blade condition, reading fractions, inside measurements, and consistent reference points matter.

Chalk Line

Creates long straight layout lines. Keep the line controlled and protect finished surfaces.

Story Pole

Transfers repeated heights or spacing without rereading numbers.

Marking

Use a sharp pencil, knife, crayon, or marker appropriate for the material and precision.

◼️ Layout Tools

Speed Square

Quick square cuts, angle references, and layout.

Combination Square

Adjustable square, depth gauge, and marking guide.

Framing Square

Large layout, stair and roof reference, and checking larger assemblies.

Level + Plumb

Layout depends on both horizontal and vertical reference—not appearance alone.

🔧 Gripping & Turning

Pliers

Choose slip-joint, tongue-and-groove, locking, linesman, or needle-nose based on grip and access.

Wrenches

Use the correct size to reduce slipping and rounded fasteners.

Sockets

Keep the tool square to the fastener and confirm metric versus standard.

Adjustable Tools

Useful, but slower and more likely to slip when poorly adjusted.

🪚 Cutting Tools

Utility Knife

Sharp blade, controlled direction, stable surface, and safe blade disposal.

Handsaw

Select tooth pattern and saw type for the material and cut.

Chisel

Keep edges sharp and protect hands from the strike path.

Snips / Files

Choose the correct cut direction or file pattern and secure the workpiece.

🧰 Tool Inspection

Handle

No cracks, looseness, splinters, oil saturation, or damaged grip.

Head / Jaw

Secure, aligned, and not mushroomed, chipped, or excessively worn.

Cutting Edge

Sharp enough to cut predictably; protected during storage.

Cleanliness

Remove residue that changes grip, fit, adjustment, or visibility.

🎒 Build Your Starter Toolbox

Buy by work: build the kit around real tasks instead of collecting tools with no system.

⚠️ Hand-Tool Safety

Strike Path

Keep hands, people, glass, finished surfaces, and unstable material out of the path.

Cut Direction

Cut away from the body when possible and keep the free hand outside the line.

Secure the Work

A moving workpiece changes the tool path.

Right Tool

Improvisation often creates slipping, breakage, damaged work, or injury.

🎮 Tool Choice Challenge

Remove a finish nail

Use a small puller, end-cutting pliers, or protected pry method—not uncontrolled force.

Mark a long fence line

Use stakes, string, and/or chalk line based on the surface and distance.

Tighten a hex fastener

Use the correct wrench or socket size.

Seat a finished piece

Use a rubber mallet or protected block instead of striking the finish directly.

🌎 Atlas Terms

Mushroomed Head

Deformed struck end that may chip or send fragments flying.

Story Pole

A marked stick or strip used to transfer repeated dimensions.

Plumb

Exactly vertical.

Square

A 90-degree relationship.

Strike Path

The area a tool or fragment may travel during impact.
