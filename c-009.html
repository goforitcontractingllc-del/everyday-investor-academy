🪵 Wood & Lumber

Species

Different strength, durability, appearance, movement, and workability.

Grade

Visual or machine grading indicates permitted defects and structural properties.

Moisture

Wet material moves as it dries and may affect finish or fit.

Treatment

Pressure treatment is intended for specific exposures; cuts and fasteners may need special consideration.

📄 Sheet Goods

Plywood

Cross-laminated veneers; grades and exposure ratings vary.

OSB

Engineered strands; common in sheathing when specified and protected.

MDF

Smooth and stable for interior finish work, but moisture-sensitive and dusty to machine.

Cement Board

Used in compatible tile and wet-area systems; not a substitute for every waterproofing requirement.

🔩 Screws

Thread

Coarse, fine, self-drilling, structural, masonry, and specialty threads serve different uses.

Head

Drive type and head shape affect installation and finish.

Length

Must achieve required penetration without hitting hidden hazards.

Coating

Exterior and treated-lumber exposure may require corrosion-resistant compatibility.

🔨 Nails

Shank

Smooth, ring, spiral, and specialty shanks hold differently.

Head

Framing, finish, roofing, siding, and connector nails are not interchangeable.

Coating

Bright, galvanized, stainless, and specialty coatings match different exposures.

Tool Compatibility

Use the correct angle, collation, length, and manufacturer allowance.

⚓ Anchors

Drywall Anchors

Capacity depends on anchor type, wall thickness, installation, and load direction.

Masonry Anchors

Base material, embedment, edge distance, hole cleaning, and torque matter.

Concrete Anchors

Mechanical and adhesive systems require specific installation.

Structural Connections

Use engineered or listed connectors when required; do not improvise.

🧴 Adhesives & Sealants

Substrate

Products must bond to the actual materials.

Movement

Sealants often accommodate movement; rigid adhesives may not.

Cure

Temperature, moisture, thickness, and time affect cure.

Compatibility

Some chemicals stain, soften, corrode, or fail on certain materials.

🌧️ Exterior Durability

Water

Detail drainage, flashing, gaps, slope, and drying.

Corrosion

Fasteners and connectors must match exposure and treated wood.

UV

Sunlight degrades some plastics, sealants, coatings, and finishes.

Soil Contact

Ground-contact exposure changes material and treatment requirements.

🧪 Material Compatibility

Treated Wood + Metal

Chemistry can accelerate corrosion without compatible hardware.

Dissimilar Metals

Galvanic reactions may occur in moisture.

Sealants + Surfaces

Check manufacturer compatibility and primer requirements.

Finish Systems

Primer, coating, substrate, moisture, and preparation must work together.

🎮 Fastener Selector

Exterior cedar fence board

Choose exterior-compatible fastener that resists staining and corrosion.

Heavy shelf into wood studs

Use appropriate structural or rated fasteners with adequate embedment.

Light item on drywall

Choose an anchor based on wall construction, load, and direction.

Metal connector

Use the connector manufacturer's specified fastener—not a random screw.

🌎 Atlas Terms

Embedment

Depth a fastener or anchor extends into the supporting material.

Edge Distance

Distance from a fastener or anchor to the material edge.

Corrosion Resistance

Ability to resist chemical or environmental deterioration.

Exposure Rating

Classification describing suitable moisture exposure.

Galvanic Corrosion

Corrosion caused by electrical interaction between dissimilar metals in an electrolyte.
