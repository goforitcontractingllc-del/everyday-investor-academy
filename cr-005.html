Credit Utilization

Credit utilization compares a revolving-account balance with its credit limit. It can be calculated for each card and across multiple revolving accounts.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: A person can pay every bill on time and still see score pressure when reported card balances are high compared with available limits.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

Utilization asks: how much of the available revolving credit is being used?

If a card has a $1,000 limit and a $300 reported balance, its utilization is 30 percent. The calculation is balance divided by limit.

Your statement balance is often—but not always—the balance that gets reported. Reporting dates can differ from payment due dates.

There is no universal magic percentage that guarantees a score result. Lower revolving utilization is generally less risky, but the entire report matters.

🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Per-card utilization

Reported balance divided by that card's limit.

Overall utilization

Total reported revolving balances divided by total revolving limits.

Statement date

The date the billing cycle closes.

Due date

The date the required payment must be received.

Credit limit

The maximum revolving amount currently available.

Reported balance

The balance supplied to the credit bureau.

💵 Numbers in Action

Utilization example

Card A
$400 balance ÷ $1,000 limit = 40%
Card B
$100 balance ÷ $2,000 limit = 5%
Overall
$500 ÷ $3,000 = 16.7%
Lesson
Both overall and individual-card patterns can matter.
⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Confusing due date with reporting date.
  • Maxing one card while overall utilization looks moderate.
  • Carrying interest-bearing debt because of the myth that a balance is required.
  • Making a payment without checking whether pending charges will post.
  • Closing a card without considering loss of available credit.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. Write each revolving account's limit and current balance.
  2. Calculate utilization for every card and overall.
  3. Check statements to identify cycle-closing dates.
  4. Pay on time first; then target high-utilization cards as cash allows.
  5. Do not create financial hardship solely to chase a tiny score change.
