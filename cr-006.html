Length of Credit History

Length of credit history looks at how long accounts have existed and how much experience your report shows. It grows mainly through time and consistent account management.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: Closing an old account or opening several new ones can change the age profile of a credit file, but age is only one part of the full scoring picture.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

Credit age is not just the age of your oldest card.

Scoring models may consider the age of your oldest account, newest account, average account age, and how long particular account types have been used.

A closed account does not necessarily disappear immediately. Positive closed accounts may remain on reports for years, depending on reporting practices.

Do not keep an expensive or unsafe account open only for age. Fees, fraud risk, debt temptation, and practical usefulness also matter.

⚙️ How the System Works
First account opensYour measurable credit history begins.
Accounts ageTime and payment experience accumulate.
New account opensThe newest-account age and average age may change.
Accounts closeHistory may remain, but available credit or future age effects can change.
🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Oldest account

How long the earliest reported account has existed.

Newest account

How recently the latest account opened.

Average age

The combined age pattern across accounts.

Account activity

How recently particular accounts were used.

Open accounts

Accounts that can still be used or are being repaid.

Closed accounts

Accounts no longer open but potentially still reported.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Opening unnecessary accounts just to create history.
  • Keeping a high-fee card solely because it is old.
  • Closing multiple cards without checking utilization effects.
  • Assuming a closed positive account vanishes the same day.
  • Expecting age to improve quickly through a trick.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. List the open date, annual fee, limit, balance, and purpose of every account.
  2. Identify the oldest useful no-fee accounts.
  3. Avoid unnecessary account openings before major financing.
  4. Consider utilization, fees, and risk—not age alone—before closing.
  5. Let time and consistent payments do the work.
