Charge-Offs

A charge-off is an accounting action showing that a creditor has classified a seriously delinquent account as unlikely to be collected normally. It does not automatically erase the debt.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: People often think 'charged off' means forgiven. The balance may still be collected, sold, settled, sued upon, or updated on credit reports.

🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

Charged off does not necessarily mean canceled or no longer owed.

After extended delinquency, a creditor may move the account out of its active receivables and classify it as a charge-off.

The original creditor may continue collecting, assign the account, or sell it to a debt buyer.

A credit report can sometimes show both the original charged-off account and a separate collection account, but balances and ownership should be reviewed carefully to avoid misleading duplication.

🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Charge-off

An accounting classification after serious delinquency.

Balance owed

The amount the creditor or owner says remains due.

Sold account

A debt transferred to a buyer.

Assigned account

A collector works for the owner without buying it.

Settlement

An agreement to accept less than the claimed full balance.

Cancellation of debt

A separate tax and legal concept that may involve a Form 1099-C.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Assuming charge-off means the balance is zero.
  • Paying a collector without confirming ownership.
  • Ignoring the original account while reviewing a collection entry.
  • Believing settlement automatically deletes accurate history.
  • Ignoring possible tax consequences of canceled debt.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. Read the original creditor's report entry.
  2. Identify the current owner and collector.
  3. Compare the balance reported by each company.
  4. Request written terms before paying or settling.
  5. Keep proof of payment, settlement, and updated balances permanently.
