Credit Disputes

A credit dispute is a formal request to investigate information you believe is inaccurate or incomplete. Strong disputes identify the exact error and include supporting evidence.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: Federal law gives consumers the right to dispute inaccurate or incomplete report information. Current CFPB guidance says consumers should dispute with both the reporting company and the company that furnished the information.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

A dispute is not a request to remove accurate information because it is harmful.

The strongest dispute says what is wrong, why it is wrong, what correction is requested, and which documents support the claim.

Keep copies of the report, dispute, evidence, delivery proof, and investigation result.

⚙️ How the System Works
Find the errorCircle the exact report entry.

Dispute both sidesSend to the bureau and furnisher.
Review resultCompare the new report with the requested correction.
🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Credit reporting company

The bureau that compiled the report.

Furnisher

The company that supplied the disputed information.

Evidence

Statements, letters, court records, identity documents, or payment proof.

Investigation result

The response explaining whether the item changed, remained, or was removed.

Frivolous dispute

A dispute that lacks enough specific information or repeats unsupported claims.

Consumer statement

A brief statement that may be added after an unresolved bureau dispute.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Disputing everything at once with no explanation.
  • Using template language that does not match the facts.
  • Sending original documents instead of copies.
  • Filing a CFPB complaint before completing the required bureau dispute process.
  • Discarding the investigation results.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. Download and save the report containing the error.
  2. Circle the exact item.
  3. Write a short factual explanation.
  4. Attach copies of supporting documents.
  5. Dispute with the bureau and furnisher, then save every response.
