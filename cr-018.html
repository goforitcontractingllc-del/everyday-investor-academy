Medical and Student Debt

Medical debt and student debt can both affect credit and collections, but they follow different contracts, reporting practices, repayment programs, and legal rules.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: A medical bill may involve insurance processing, provider billing, financial assistance, and collection. A student loan may involve a servicer, federal benefits, private-loan terms, delinquency, or default.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

For medical bills, confirm the provider, services, insurance adjustments, financial-assistance options, and collector before paying.

For federal student loans, use StudentAid.gov and the official loan servicer or Default Resolution Group—not a company charging for access to federal options.

Reporting rules and industry practices for medical debt can change, so verify the current status of any item directly on all three reports.

🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Explanation of benefits

An insurer's explanation of what was billed, allowed, and paid.

Financial assistance

Provider programs that may reduce qualifying medical bills.

Federal student loan

Education debt governed by federal program rules.

Private student loan

Education debt governed mainly by the lender contract and applicable law.

Delinquency

A loan payment that is past due.

Default

A more serious status reached under the applicable loan rules.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Paying a medical collector before confirming insurance adjustments.
  • Paying a company for federal student-loan help available through official channels.
  • Refinancing federal loans without understanding lost federal protections.
  • Ignoring default notices.
  • Assuming a report change automatically means the underlying bill vanished.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. Identify whether each debt is medical, federal student, or private student debt.
  2. Match the debt to statements and official account records.
  3. Check assistance or repayment options before using new debt.
  4. Dispute inaccuracies with evidence.
  5. Save all agreements and updated balances.
