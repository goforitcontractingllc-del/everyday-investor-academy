Business Credit

Business credit is credit used primarily for a business purpose. A business may build its own credit profile, but lenders can still review the owner's personal credit or require a personal guarantee.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: Separating business and personal systems improves bookkeeping and can help a business establish its own financial identity. It does not automatically protect the owner from every debt or eliminate personal underwriting.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

Begin with a properly registered business, consistent business information, an EIN when required, and a dedicated business bank account.

Open credit only when it serves a real business need and the business cash flow can support repayment.

Ask exactly which business reporting agencies receive payment history and whether the lender requires a personal guarantee.

🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Business credit profile

Reported information connected to the company rather than only the individual owner.

EIN

A federal employer identification number used for tax and business identification purposes.

Personal guarantee

The owner's agreement to become personally responsible if the business does not pay.

Trade credit

Payment terms provided by a supplier or vendor.

Business card

A revolving account intended primarily for business expenses.

Business credit bureau

A company that collects and reports commercial payment information.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Believing an LLC automatically creates strong business credit.
  • Opening accounts only to manufacture a score.
  • Ignoring personal guarantees.
  • Mixing household and company expenses.
  • Assuming every vendor reports payment history.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. Verify the legal business name, address, and registration.
  2. Use a dedicated business bank account and bookkeeping system.
  3. Check existing business credit files for errors.
  4. Ask vendors and lenders where they report.
  5. Borrow only for a documented business purpose with a repayment plan.
