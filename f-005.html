Score an Opportunity Before It Scores You

Evaluate fit, timing, cost, risk, learning value, and exit before saying yes.

ADHD-friendly Practical 15–25 minutes Printable
Real-life opening

Why this matters

Opportunities can feel urgent because someone else wants an answer. A job, partnership, property, course, loan, or investment may look valuable while quietly demanding time, cash, skills, or risk you do not have.

Quick answer

A good opportunity fits your goal, capacity, timing, values, and exit—not just your excitement.

Deep dive

Open only what you need.

The full lesson is here, but you do not have to read it in one sitting.

Does this move the life or project you actually want, or only distract you with motion?

Do you have the time, money, health, tools, skills, support, and attention required?

What happens if it underperforms? Can you stop, sell, renegotiate, pause, or recover?

Sometimes an opportunity is worth less profit because it builds a valuable skill or relationship—but name that trade clearly.

Common mistakes

What usually goes wrong

Trying to solve everything

Too many open loops create paralysis. Reduce the lesson to one current decision.

Using assumptions as facts

Write assumptions down so they can be verified instead of silently controlling the decision.

Waiting for perfect certainty

Most real decisions require a reasonable next step, not complete knowledge.

Skipping the record

Notes, screenshots, calculations, and dates protect memory and improve future decisions.

Real-life challenge

Use this today

Score one real opportunity. Do not decide from the total alone—write the lowest category and what would improve it.
