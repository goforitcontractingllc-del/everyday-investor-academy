Build Better Questions

Turn vague confusion into questions that produce useful answers.

ADHD-friendlyRyan-readable15–25 minutesPrintable
Real-life opening

Why this matters

Most bad research starts with a question that is too broad. “How do I buy land?” can send you through hundreds of videos. “What must I verify before making an offer on a rural parcel in Jackson County?” gives your brain a target.

Quick answer

A strong question names the decision, location, constraint, and kind of answer you need.

Practical note: Ryan-readable rule: ask the question so another person could answer it without first listening to a ten-minute backstory.
Deep dive

Open only what you need.

The lesson is complete, but it is broken into manageable layers.

Ask what decision the answer will support. Information without a decision often becomes endless browsing.

Include location, timeline, budget, role, and limitation when they change the answer.

A legal question, price question, process question, and personal preference question need different sources.

End research with something concrete: which agency, document, measurement, quote, or professional confirms the answer?

Common mistakes

What usually goes wrong

Moving before defining

Activity feels productive, but a vague target creates wasted effort.

Using only one perspective

Important decisions often need legal, financial, practical, and personal views.

Ignoring capacity

A good idea can still be wrong for the current time, money, health, or workload.

Not recording the answer

Save the result, date, source, and next action so the work does not disappear.

Real-life challenge

Use this today

Rewrite one vague question you have into a version that includes the decision, location, constraint, and verifying source.
