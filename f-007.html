Research Without Getting Lost

Use a research trail that keeps facts, sources, dates, and next actions organized.

ADHD-friendlyRyan-readable15–25 minutesPrintable
Real-life opening

Why this matters

Research can feel productive while quietly becoming avoidance. Ten open tabs are not the same as an answer. A good research process tells you what you found, where it came from, how current it is, and what still needs confirmation.

Quick answer

Research is finished when you can support a decision—not when you have consumed everything available.

Practical note: Field rule: if you cannot explain what a link proves, it is not yet useful research.
Deep dive

Open only what you need.

The lesson is complete, but it is broken into manageable layers.

Start with official agencies, original documents, contracts, statutes, product instructions, or primary records when accuracy matters.

Programs, fees, laws, forms, course approvals, and agency procedures can change. Record when you verified them.

Do not save only a link. Write the specific fact the source supports.

Research until the important unknowns are controlled enough for the next step—not until all uncertainty disappears.

Common mistakes

What usually goes wrong

Moving before defining

Activity feels productive, but a vague target creates wasted effort.

Using only one perspective

Important decisions often need legal, financial, practical, and personal views.

Ignoring capacity

A good idea can still be wrong for the current time, money, health, or workload.

Not recording the answer

Save the result, date, source, and next action so the work does not disappear.

Real-life challenge

Use this today

Take one saved link and write the exact claim it supports, the date you checked it, and what it does not prove.
