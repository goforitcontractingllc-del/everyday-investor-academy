Learn to Close Loops

Finish decisions, messages, payments, records, and follow-ups instead of carrying them mentally.

ADHD-friendlyRyan-readable15–25 minutesPrintable
Real-life opening

Why this matters

An open loop is anything your brain believes still needs attention. Some are real tasks. Others are waiting items, unresolved decisions, or things that should be intentionally dropped.

Quick answer

Every open loop needs one status: do, schedule, wait, delegate, decide, or close.

Practical note: Loop rule: if nobody owns the next action, the task is not organized.
Deep dive

Open only what you need.

Who has the next move— you, another person, or an agency?

Waiting without a follow-up date keeps the loop mentally active.

Some items are completed. Others are canceled, declined, archived, or no longer worth the cost.

Common mistakes

What usually goes wrong

Responding to volume

The loudest request is not always the most consequential.

Leaving ownership unclear

Tasks stay open when nobody knows who has the next move.

Trusting memory alone

Important dates, changes, money, and agreements need a record.

Overloading the week

Planning without capacity creates repeated failure and discouragement.

Real-life challenge

Use this today

Write every open loop you can think of for five minutes. Give each one a status and owner.
