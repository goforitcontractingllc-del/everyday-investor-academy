Housing Types and How They Actually Work

Compare apartments, houses, condos, manufactured homes, ADUs, cottage clusters, duplexes, rural property, and shared housing by how they function—not just how they look.

Beginner friendlyInteractive20–30 minutesPrintable workbook
Real-life opening

Why this matters

Two homes can have the same number of bedrooms and completely different rules, costs, responsibilities, privacy, maintenance, and financing. The structure is only one part of the housing system.

Quick answer

Choose housing by matching the legal setup, total cost, maintenance load, location, space, and future flexibility to the life you actually live.

Real-world tip: compare how the housing operates on a hard Tuesday—not how it photographs on a good Saturday.
Deep dive

Open only what you need.

The complete lesson is divided into short, expandable sections.

Apartments usually trade private control for lower maintenance responsibility and shared systems. Ask about utilities, parking, laundry, guests, pets, noise, renewals, and management responsiveness.

A detached house may offer privacy, yard space, storage, and control, but often comes with higher utility, transportation, repair, and upkeep costs.

Ownership may include shared walls, common areas, dues, rules, insurance layers, and association responsibilities. Review governing documents and current costs.

The home and the land may be owned together or separately. Space rent, park rules, financing, title, installation, and resale can change the economics.

An accessory dwelling unit can create flexibility for family, rental income, or smaller living, but local rules, utilities, access, parking, and privacy matter.

These arrangements can create smaller detached homes with shared common space. Ownership, maintenance, parking, affordability, and local planning standards vary.

Sharing a structure can reduce land cost per household and create income options, but noise, utilities, maintenance, and management become more connected.

Rural housing may offer land and privacy but create access, well, septic, wildfire, transportation, internet, and maintenance demands. Roommates and co-housing require clear agreements.

Common mistakes

What people often miss

Comparing only one number

Total cost, risk, time, rules, and responsibilities can matter more than the advertised price.

Assuming the setup is standard

Ownership, rental terms, services, utilities, and local rules vary.

Relying on verbal promises

Important terms, repairs, payments, and conditions need a written record.

Waiting to document

Records are strongest when created close to the event.
