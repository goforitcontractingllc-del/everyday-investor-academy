Housing Looks Different

There is no single correct housing type. Different homes solve different needs, costs, locations, and stages of life.

Start readingCheck current information

Housing is not one shape

A detached house is only one housing form. Some people need privacy and land. Others need lower maintenance, smaller spaces, shared services, accessible design, or a location close to work and transit.

Detached homeMore separation and control, usually more maintenance and land responsibility.
ApartmentShared building and management, often lower personal maintenance responsibility.
CondoIndividual ownership inside a shared community with association rules and fees.
Manufactured homeFactory-built housing that may sit on owned land or rented space.
ADUA smaller secondary home on the same property as a primary dwelling.
Cottage clusterSeveral compact homes organized as a coordinated community, often around shared open space.

The best housing type depends on the goal

A first-time renter, single parent, retiree, contractor, investor, disabled resident, and multigenerational family may all evaluate the same property differently.

Tradeoffs matter

  • Privacy versus shared responsibility
  • Lower purchase price versus association fees
  • More land versus more maintenance
  • Smaller space versus better location
  • Ownership control versus flexibility
Better question: Not “Which housing type is best?” Ask “Which housing type fits the person, budget, responsibilities, location, and next stage?”

How to compare housing types

Compare total monthly cost, maintenance, rules, accessibility, privacy, parking, storage, outdoor space, location, resale or rental flexibility, and the amount of control the resident or owner actually has.
