Deposits, Move-In Proof, and Protecting Your Money

Create a condition record, photo system, payment trail, repair log, and move-out file that protects your memory and your money.

Beginner friendlyInteractive20–30 minutesPrintable workbook
Real-life opening

Why this matters

Most move-in details feel obvious in the first week. Months later, the same scratch, stain, missing screen, or damaged cabinet can become a dispute about when it happened and who caused it.

Quick answer

Document condition before settling in, report problems in writing, save payment proof, and build the move-out file from the first day.

Real-world tip: the best move-out evidence is created at move-in.
Deep dive

Open only what you need.

The complete lesson is divided into short, expandable sections.

Record the name, amount, purpose, date, refundability, and receipt for every deposit, fee, and prepaid amount.

Walk every room, open cabinets, test fixtures, note odors, stains, damage, pests, moisture, and missing items.

Take wide shots for location and close shots for detail. Include date context and keep the original files.

Use the required form or written process and keep proof that it was delivered.

State the issue, location, date noticed, effect, urgency, access availability, and supporting photos.

Save approvals, maintenance visits, damage events, communications, and receipts.

Review the agreement, notice, cleaning, keys, forwarding address, inspection, and documentation requirements.

Keep the lease, addenda, condition report, photos, payment proof, repair records, move-out evidence, and deposit accounting together.

Common mistakes

What people often miss

Comparing only one number

Total cost, risk, time, rules, and responsibilities can matter more than the advertised price.

Assuming the setup is standard

Ownership, rental terms, services, utilities, and local rules vary.

Relying on verbal promises

Important terms, repairs, payments, and conditions need a written record.

Waiting to document

Records are strongest when created close to the event.
