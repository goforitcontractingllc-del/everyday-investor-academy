Landlord and Property-Manager Responsibilities

Understand how property operations, maintenance, communication, access, records, safety, and legal responsibilities fit together.

Beginner friendlyInteractive20–30 minutesPrintable workbook
Real-life opening

Why this matters

Most housing problems are not caused by one broken item. They grow when no one knows who owns the next step, when access is unclear, when repairs are not documented, or when responsibility is assumed instead of confirmed.

Quick answer

A strong housing operation has a clear reporting system, documented decisions, lawful access, prioritized repairs, and a record showing what happened next.

Real-world tip: every maintenance request needs an owner, status, access plan, and next update date.
Deep dive

Open only what you need.

The complete lesson is divided into short, expandable sections.

The lease, addenda, management agreement, and current law divide duties among owner, manager, tenant, vendors, and agencies.

A repair process needs intake, urgency screening, access, assignment, documentation, completion, and follow-up.

Active leaks, electrical danger, heating failure, structural problems, sewage, fire risk, and security failures may require faster response than cosmetic issues.

Entry depends on purpose, notice, emergency, consent, and current law. Poor access records create conflict and delays.

A good work order states location, condition, access, urgency, expected result, photos, and authority limits.

The resident should know the request was received, what happens next, who is responsible, and when to follow up.

Save requests, notices, invoices, photos, vendor notes, approvals, denials, completion records, and resident communications.

Inspections, filters, gutters, drainage, smoke alarms, leak checks, and seasonal preparation reduce larger failures.

Common mistakes

What people often miss

Using outdated information

Housing law, programs, forms, eligibility, and local procedures can change.

Skipping the written record

Notices, requests, payments, decisions, and follow-ups need evidence.

Confusing different systems

Legal rights, assistance programs, lease duties, and operational practices are not interchangeable.

Waiting until the deadline

Housing problems become harder when documents, help, and options are gathered too late.
