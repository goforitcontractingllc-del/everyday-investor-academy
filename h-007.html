Down Payments and Closing Costs

The down payment is only one part of the money needed to buy a home. Cash to close also includes transaction costs, prepaid expenses, and reserve needs.

20–30 minute readBeginner friendlyEvergreen core

Down payment and cash to close are different

The down payment is the buyer's ownership contribution toward the purchase price. Cash to close is the total amount the buyer must bring after credits, deposits, and other adjustments are included.

Core idea: A buyer can save enough for a down payment and still be short of the actual money needed to close.

Common upfront costs

Earnest moneyA good-faith deposit submitted with or after an accepted offer and later credited according to the contract.
InspectionPayment for a general home inspection and any specialized inspections.
AppraisalA lender-required opinion of value in many financed purchases.
Loan costsOrigination, underwriting, processing, discount points, or other lender charges.
Title and escrowCharges related to title review, insurance, settlement, and closing administration.
PrepaidsMoney collected in advance for insurance, taxes, and interest.

Down payment percentages

Different loan programs allow different minimum down payments. A lower down payment may preserve cash but can increase the monthly payment, mortgage insurance, rate, or lender risk. A larger down payment may reduce borrowing but should not leave the buyer without reserves.

Seller credits and lender credits

Credits can reduce some buyer closing costs, but they do not make the cost disappear. A seller credit may be built into the negotiated price. A lender credit may come with a higher interest rate. The buyer should compare the long-term tradeoff.

Learning example

Purchase price: $350,000

Possible categories include a down payment, inspection and appraisal, lender charges, title and escrow, prepaid insurance and taxes, and initial reserves. The exact amounts vary by loan, property, location, and negotiated terms.

Do not empty the account at closing

A new owner may immediately face utility deposits, moving expenses, locks, tools, appliances, repairs, furniture, landscaping, or an insurance deductible. The healthiest plan keeps money available after closing.

Questions to ask the lender

  • What is the estimated total cash to close?
  • Which costs can change before closing?
  • Are discount points included?
  • Is mortgage insurance required?
  • How much will be placed in escrow?
  • What seller or lender credits are permitted?
  • What reserves should remain after closing?
