Roommates, Shared Housing, and Written Agreements

Divide money, chores, guests, privacy, utilities, damage, and move-out duties before conflict starts.

Beginner friendlyInteractive20–30 minutesPrintable workbook
Real-life opening

Why this matters

Shared housing can make housing possible, but unclear expectations can turn small issues into financial and legal problems.

Quick answer

Divide money, chores, guests, privacy, utilities, damage, and move-out duties before conflict starts.

Deep dive

Open only what you need.

Clarify whether the setup is co-tenancy, subletting, owner-occupancy, or another arrangement.

Write who pays rent, utilities, deposits, groceries, internet, and late charges.

Agree on guests, quiet hours, pets, smoking, parking, food, and cleaning.

Define bedrooms, storage, communication, mail, and access.

Photograph condition and identify who owns shared items.

Choose how concerns are raised and when outside help is needed.

Write notice, cleaning, keys, deposit, and final-bill expectations.

A roommate agreement does not override the lease or current law.
