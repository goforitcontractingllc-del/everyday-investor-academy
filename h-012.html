Condos and HOAs

A condominium separates ownership of the individual unit from ownership and responsibility for shared property, while an HOA creates rules, fees, and community obligations.

20–30 minute readBeginner friendlyEvergreen core

What a condo owner owns

Condominium ownership usually includes the interior unit plus an ownership interest in common elements such as roofs, halls, exterior walls, landscaping, parking, or amenities.

Core idea: Condo ownership is private ownership inside a shared legal and financial system.

What the association does

The association collects assessments, maintains common property, buys insurance, enforces rules, plans reserves, and manages community decisions.

Documents matter

DeclarationCreates the condominium and defines ownership and restrictions.
BylawsExplain governance, meetings, voting, and board authority.
RulesCover daily use, parking, pets, noise, rentals, and appearance.
Budget and reservesShow current costs and preparation for future repairs.

Assessments and special assessments

Regular assessments support ongoing operations. Special assessments may be charged when major work exceeds available reserves.

Questions before buying

  • What exactly is included in the monthly fee?
  • How strong are the reserves?
  • Are major repairs planned?
  • Are there pending lawsuits?
  • Are rentals restricted?
  • What insurance must the owner carry?
  • Are there repeated rule disputes?

Condo versus HOA

Not every HOA is a condominium. Detached homes can belong to an HOA while the owners remain responsible for their own buildings and lots.

Practical rule: Never buy only the unit. You are also buying into the association's finances, maintenance history, rules, and decision-making culture.
