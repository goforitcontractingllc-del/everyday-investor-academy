Townhomes

Townhomes are attached homes that usually provide individual entrances and vertical living space while sharing one or more walls with neighboring homes.

20–30 minute readBeginner friendlyEvergreen core

What a townhome is

A townhome is typically a multi-level attached dwelling with its own entrance. Ownership can be structured as fee-simple ownership, condominium ownership, rental housing, or another arrangement.

Why ownership structure matters

Two townhomes can look identical but carry different responsibilities. One owner may own the building and lot. Another may own only the interior unit while an association maintains the exterior.

QuestionWhy it matters
Who owns the roof and exterior?Determines repair responsibility and insurance needs.
Who owns the land?Affects control, maintenance, and legal structure.
Are utilities shared?May affect billing, repairs, and access.
What does the HOA cover?Changes the true monthly cost.

Benefits

  • More home-like privacy than many apartments
  • Smaller exterior footprint
  • Efficient land use
  • Often lower exterior maintenance than detached homes

Tradeoffs

  • Shared walls and noise transfer
  • Stairs and accessibility concerns
  • Association rules or fees
  • Shared roofs, drainage, or utilities
  • Limited parking and storage

Construction lens

Fire separation, sound control, flashing, roof transitions, shared walls, drainage, and maintenance access are especially important in attached housing.

Investor lens

Rental restrictions, HOA rules, fees, insurance, and shared-building repairs can strongly affect returns.
