Tiny Homes

Tiny homes reduce square footage, but they do not remove the need for legal placement, utilities, sanitation, access, safety, and a workable ownership model.

20–30 minute readBeginner friendlyEvergreen core

What counts as a tiny home?

“Tiny home” is a popular description, not one universal legal category. A tiny home may be built on a permanent foundation, constructed on wheels, manufactured, modular, or used as an accessory dwelling.

Core idea: Small size does not automatically make a home legal, movable, affordable, or easy to place.

The big classification questions

Foundation or wheels?Changes how the structure may be regulated, financed, and placed.
Primary home or accessory?Affects zoning, density, and utility rules.
Permanent or temporary?Changes occupancy, permits, and placement.
Owned land or rented space?Changes control, cost, and long-term stability.

Land and utility reality

A tiny home still needs legal access, water, wastewater, power, drainage, fire access, parking, setbacks, and sometimes engineering or foundation work.

Why tiny does not always mean cheap

  • Custom construction costs per square foot can be high.
  • Land may cost more than the structure.
  • Utility connections can be expensive.
  • Special trailers, transport, cranes, and setup may be required.
  • Financing and insurance can be limited.

Design matters more in less space

Storage, stairs, lofts, ventilation, moisture, appliance size, accessibility, privacy, and everyday movement must be planned carefully.

Good uses

  • Accessory dwelling
  • Rural or family housing where legally allowed
  • Small-home community
  • Temporary or transitional use where permitted
  • Low-maintenance ownership for the right household
Best first question: “Where can this legally and safely go?” comes before “Which tiny home should I buy?”
