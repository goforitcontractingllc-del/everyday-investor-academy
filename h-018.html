Home Inspections and Due Diligence

Understand inspection scope, condition evidence, specialist referrals, repair decisions, and what an inspection does not guarantee.

Beginner friendlyInteractive20–30 minutesPrintable workbook
Real-life opening

Why this matters

A home inspection can reveal visible conditions and warning signs, but it cannot eliminate all uncertainty or replace specialist evaluation.

Quick answer

Understand inspection scope, condition evidence, specialist referrals, repair decisions, and what an inspection does not guarantee.

Deep dive

Open only what you need.

Read the inspector's agreement and understand exclusions and limitations.

Seeing conditions and asking questions can add context beyond the report.

Some items are urgent safety or structural issues; others are routine care.

Roof, sewer, electrical, structural, pest, environmental, septic, well, or other specialists may be needed.

Do not focus only on the summary page.

Repair cost includes investigation, access, permits, hidden damage, and timing.

Purchase agreements may create inspection and response deadlines.

Negotiate, request repair, change price, accept risk, or walk away according to the contract and advice.
