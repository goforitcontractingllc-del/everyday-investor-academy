Investing District · I-001D

Practice, Numbers, and Knowledge Check

Apply the investing foundation to a simple property scenario without pretending the first calculation tells the whole story.

Version 2Deep-dive lessonLiving Atlas connected

Scenario

A small house costs $250,000. Expected rent is $1,850 per month. The property needs $20,000 in repairs. Taxes, insurance, maintenance, vacancy, management, and financing still need to be estimated.

First-pass questions

  • What is the total cash needed?
  • What is realistic gross income?
  • What operating expenses are missing?
  • What loan payment is expected?
  • What happens during vacancy?
  • What repairs could exceed the budget?

Gross scheduled rent

$1,850 × 12 = $22,200 annually.

Not profit

The $22,200 must still absorb vacancy, operating costs, debt service, and capital needs.

Practice checklist

  • Write the investment goal.
  • List every expected return channel.
  • List every known and unknown cost.
  • Write the worst realistic outcome.
  • Identify the exit plan.

Quiz

Question: Why is gross rent not the same as cash flow?

Open answer

Because vacancy, repairs, taxes, insurance, management, utilities, reserves, financing, and other costs must be paid before cash flow exists.
