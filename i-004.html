Deal Analysis Before You Buy

A deal should be tested with realistic numbers before emotion, urgency, or sales pressure takes over.

18–30 minutes Beginner friendly Detailed lesson Atlas terms included
Why this matters: Most bad deals do not become bad at closing. They were already bad when assumptions were left untested.
📖 The Lesson

Start with the total project

The purchase price is only one number. A complete analysis includes acquisition, closing, financing, repairs, holding costs, operations, reserves, and exit costs.

Core analysis terms

NOI

Income after operating expenses, before debt service.

Cap Rate

NOI divided by property value.

Cash-on-Cash Return

Annual pre-tax cash flow divided by cash invested.

ROI

Gain or profit compared with total investment.

Run three versions

  1. Expected case: what you reasonably believe will happen.
  2. Bad case: lower income, higher cost, slower timeline.
  3. Very bad case: major repair, vacancy, financing issue, or delayed sale.
Decision rule: If the deal only works when every assumption is perfect, it does not work.

💵 Numbers in Action

Simple deal snapshot

Purchase
$250,000
Repairs
$25,000
Annual NOI
$18,000
Cash invested
$55,000

These numbers are not enough by themselves. Financing, debt service, reserves, and exit costs still matter.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Using asking rent instead of verified market rent.
  • Leaving out vacancy, maintenance, management, or reserves.
  • Believing the seller's repair estimate.
  • Ignoring selling costs in a flip.
📝 Try It Yourself
  • Create a full list of acquisition costs.
  • Build expected, bad, and very bad cases.
  • Calculate NOI.
  • Write the deal's main failure point.
