Deal Analysis Before You Buy

Deal analysis converts a property story into numbers. It tests the price, income, expenses, financing, repairs, reserves, and exit assumptions before money becomes difficult to recover.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: A deal can look profitable because one expense, repair, vacancy assumption, or closing cost was omitted. Analysis is designed to expose missing information before closing.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

Start with verified income, not the seller’s best-case projection.

Estimate normal operating expenses, vacancy, reserves, financing, closing costs, and immediate repairs.

Run more than one scenario: expected, conservative, and failure case.

Clickable terms: Cash-on-Cash Return, Capitalization Rate, Effective Gross Income
⚙️ How the System Works
Verify property dataRent roll, leases, taxes, insurance, utilities, and condition.
Build the base caseUse realistic income and expenses.
Stress testIncrease vacancy, repairs, rates, and holding time.
Set the decisionBuy, renegotiate, investigate further, or walk away.
🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Purchase basis

Price plus acquisition costs and certain capitalized costs.

Effective income

Expected income after vacancy and collection loss.

Operating expense ratio

Operating expenses compared with effective income.

Cap rate

NOI divided by property value or price.

Cash-on-cash return

Annual pre-tax cash flow divided by actual cash invested.

Break-even point

The occupancy or income level needed to cover required costs.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Using pro forma rent as current rent.
  • Leaving out management because you plan to self-manage.
  • Ignoring closing and financing costs.
  • Using one optimistic repair estimate.
  • Failing to test vacancy or interest-rate changes.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. Collect leases, rent roll, tax records, insurance quotes, and utility history.
  2. Build expected and conservative scenarios.
  3. Calculate NOI, cash flow, and cash-on-cash return.
  4. Add immediate repairs and reserves.
  5. Write the maximum acceptable price.
🧪 Knowledge Check

1. Should projected rent be treated as verified current rent?

2. Does cash-on-cash return use actual cash invested?

3. Should a deal be stress-tested?
