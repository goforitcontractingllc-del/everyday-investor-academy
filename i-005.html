Due Diligence and Risk

Due diligence is the process of verifying the physical, legal, financial, and operational truth before obligations become permanent.

18–30 minutes Beginner friendly Detailed lesson Atlas terms included
Why this matters: Listings and seller statements are starting points, not proof. Due diligence protects you from buying assumptions.
📖 The Lesson

Four due-diligence layers

Physical

Condition, systems, utilities, access, drainage, repairs, and hazards.

Legal

Title, zoning, permits, leases, unit count, easements, and restrictions.

Financial

Income, expenses, taxes, insurance, debt, reserves, and unpaid obligations.

Operational

Residents, vendors, maintenance history, records, policies, and management quality.

Documents to review

  • Title report and recorded documents
  • Permit history and certificates of occupancy
  • Leases and rent roll
  • Tax and insurance records
  • Utility bills
  • Repair invoices and warranties
  • Association documents, if applicable

Risk is not only “what could happen”

Risk also includes how likely the event is, how expensive it would be, how quickly it could happen, and whether you can survive it. This is risk capacity, not merely risk tolerance.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Relying only on listing information.
  • Skipping permit and legal-unit verification.
  • Assuming insurance will be available at the expected cost.
  • Using the inspection contingency without enough time for specialists.
📝 Try It Yourself
  • Create a physical, legal, financial, and operational checklist.
  • Find the county assessor and GIS page for a sample property.
  • List three specialists the property may need.
  • Write the largest risk you could not afford.
