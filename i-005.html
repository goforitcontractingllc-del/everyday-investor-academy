Due Diligence and Risk

Due diligence is the organized investigation completed before buying, lending, partnering, or committing major money. It tests the property’s physical, legal, financial, operational, and market reality.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: Most investment failures are not caused by one dramatic surprise. They are often created by several smaller facts that were never verified.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

Physical due diligence examines condition, systems, repairs, environmental concerns, and deferred maintenance.

Legal due diligence examines title, zoning, leases, permits, easements, liens, and ownership authority.

Financial due diligence examines income, expenses, taxes, insurance, financing, reserves, and seller records.

Clickable terms: Due Diligence, Title Exception, Red Flag
⚙️ How the System Works
Create the checklistList every fact that must be verified.
Gather primary recordsUse official documents, inspections, and direct evidence.
Resolve red flagsPrice, repair, insure, renegotiate, or walk away.
Preserve the fileKeep every report, estimate, approval, and decision.
🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Title review

Ownership, liens, easements, restrictions, and exceptions.

Property inspection

Condition of structure, systems, site, and safety.

Lease audit

Rent, deposits, terms, notices, concessions, and resident obligations.

Permit review

Whether work and use were lawfully approved.

Market review

Comparable rents, sales, supply, demand, and exit risk.

Fraud review

Identity, authority, ownership, documents, and use of investor funds.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Relying only on seller-provided documents.
  • Skipping permit and zoning research.
  • Assuming title insurance replaces all investigation.
  • Sending investor money before verifying ownership and authority.
  • Ignoring uncomfortable facts because time or money has already been spent.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. Build physical, legal, financial, market, and operational checklists.
  2. Verify ownership, title, zoning, permits, and leases.
  3. Use independent inspectors and written estimates.
  4. Confirm insurance and financing before deadlines expire.
  5. Document the final go, renegotiate, or walk-away decision.
🧪 Knowledge Check
