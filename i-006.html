Financing an Investment Property

Financing changes the return, risk, cash requirement, and survivability of an investment.

18–30 minutes Beginner friendly Detailed lesson Atlas terms included
Why this matters: Leverage can increase returns, but it also creates fixed obligations that remain even when income falls or repairs rise.
📖 The Lesson

Financing is part of the deal

The loan is not separate from the investment. Rate, term, down payment, fees, reserves, recourse, and refinance assumptions all shape the result.

Main financing paths

Owner-Occupied Financing

May apply to house hacking when the buyer genuinely occupies the property.

Conventional Investor Loan

Typically requires stronger down payment and reserve capacity than owner-occupied loans.

DSCR Loan

May rely heavily on the property's debt-service coverage ratio.

Private or Bridge Financing

Shorter-term, often higher-cost money used for acquisition or rehab.

Seller Financing

The seller accepts payments under negotiated terms.

Partnership Capital

Another person contributes money, credit, labor, or guarantees in exchange for ownership or return.

Leverage

Leverage means using borrowed money to control a larger asset. It can improve cash-on-cash returns when the property performs well. It can also magnify losses and reduce flexibility.

Financing rule: Never build a permanent strategy around temporary financing without a backup exit.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Comparing only interest rates and ignoring fees.
  • Using adjustable or short-term debt without understanding reset or maturity.
  • Assuming refinance approval is guaranteed.
  • Signing a partnership without a written operating and exit agreement.
📝 Try It Yourself
  • Compare two loan structures using total cash, payment, term, and fees.
  • Calculate LTV.
  • Calculate DSCR using realistic NOI.
  • Write a backup plan if refinancing fails.
