Rental Property Income and Expenses

A rental property succeeds or fails through the gap between realistic income and complete operating costs.

18–30 minutes Beginner friendly Detailed lesson Atlas terms included
Why this matters: Rent looks simple from the outside, but the true result depends on vacancy, repairs, utilities, management, taxes, insurance, and long-term replacements.
📖 The Lesson

Start with effective income

Scheduled rent is what the leases say should be collected. Effective gross income is what remains after vacancy, nonpayment, concessions, and other income adjustments.

Operating expenses

Fixed expenses

Taxes, insurance, licenses, subscriptions, and some recurring contracts.

Variable expenses

Repairs, utilities, turnover, landscaping, pest control, and supplies.

Management

Professional management or the value of the owner's own time and systems.

Reserves

Money set aside for large replacements and unexpected failures.

Capital expenses are different

A new roof, major plumbing replacement, or HVAC system is usually not treated like an ordinary monthly repair. Investors still need to plan for these capital expenditures.

Separate property performance from financing

Operating expenses lead to NOI. Loan payments come afterward. This separation makes it easier to compare properties with different financing.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Leaving out vacancy because the unit is occupied today.
  • Using one unusually low repair year as the normal budget.
  • Ignoring the value of management time.
  • Treating every expense as tax-deductible without professional guidance.
📝 Try It Yourself
  • Create a complete annual expense list for one sample rental.
  • Separate operating expenses from debt service.
  • Estimate a realistic vacancy amount.
  • Choose one major system and estimate its future reserve need.
