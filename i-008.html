Vacancy, Turnover, and Resident Risk

Vacancy is lost income while the property still carries costs. Turnover adds cleaning, repairs, marketing, screening, utilities, and time between residents.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: A property can look profitable at full occupancy and perform poorly after realistic vacancy, nonpayment, turnover, concessions, and legal costs are included.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

Physical vacancy means the unit is empty. Economic vacancy also includes lost rent from nonpayment, concessions, or uncollectible balances.

Turnover begins before the resident leaves and ends only when the next qualified resident begins paying.

Resident risk is managed through lawful screening, clear leases, consistent communication, reserves, documentation, and fair-housing compliance.

🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Physical vacancy

Time when the unit is unoccupied.

Economic vacancy

Income loss from vacancy, concessions, or nonpayment.

Turnover cost

Cleaning, repair, utilities, marketing, and labor between residents.

Rent loss

Income not collected during downtime.

Resident screening

A lawful and consistently applied review process.

Concession

A discount or incentive reducing effective rent.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Underwriting at 100% occupancy.
  • Counting deposits as ordinary rent.
  • Using inconsistent screening standards.
  • Waiting until move-out to plan the turnover.
  • Ignoring fair-housing and notice rules.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. Use a vacancy assumption in every analysis.
  2. Track average days vacant and turn cost.
  3. Create a pre-move-out and turnover checklist.
  4. Use written screening criteria.
  5. Maintain a dedicated vacancy and turnover reserve.
🧪 Knowledge Check

1. Does economic vacancy include concessions and nonpayment?

2. Do property costs stop when a unit is empty?

3. Should screening criteria be applied consistently?
