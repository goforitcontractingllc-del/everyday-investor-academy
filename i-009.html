Reserves, Maintenance, and Capital Planning

Reserves are cash intentionally held for uncertainty. Maintenance keeps systems working, while capital planning prepares for expensive components that wear out over time.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: Positive monthly cash flow can disappear when a roof, sewer line, HVAC system, exterior, or appliance package fails. Planned reserves turn predictable aging into a managed cost.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

Operating reserves cover short-term income or expense surprises.

Capital reserves prepare for larger replacements with multi-year useful lives.

A reserve target should reflect the property's condition, age, insurance, debt, income stability, and access to emergency funding.

🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Operating reserve

Cash for temporary income loss or ordinary expense shocks.

Capital reserve

Cash held for major replacements or improvements.

Preventive maintenance

Scheduled work intended to reduce failures.

Useful life

The period a component is expected to function.

Capital expenditure

A major cost that improves or extends property life.

Reserve study

A forecast of future major components and funding needs.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Calling a credit card a reserve.
  • Using every reserve for the down payment.
  • Budgeting only for routine repairs.
  • Ignoring insurance deductibles.
  • Failing to update estimates as the property ages.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. Create a component inventory.
  2. Record condition, age, and estimated replacement year.
  3. Collect current replacement estimates.
  4. Set monthly operating and capital reserve transfers.
  5. Review the plan annually and after major work.
🧪 Knowledge Check

1. Is a credit limit the same as cash reserves?

2. Should major future replacements be planned separately?
