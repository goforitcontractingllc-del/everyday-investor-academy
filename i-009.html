Reserves, Maintenance, and Capital Planning

Reserves turn inevitable property failures into planned expenses instead of emergencies.

18–30 minutes Beginner friendly Detailed lesson Atlas terms included
Why this matters: Every roof, appliance, pipe, surface, and mechanical system has a useful life. Ignoring that fact does not make replacement optional.
📖 The Lesson

Three money buckets

Operating cash

Money for normal monthly expenses and small repairs.

Emergency reserve

Money for sudden failures, deductibles, vacancy, or income interruption.

Capital reserve

Money accumulated for predictable long-life replacements.

Project contingency

Extra money for uncertainty within a specific repair or renovation project.

Useful life planning

Investors estimate when major components may need replacement, what they may cost, and how much should be reserved each month. This is a capital plan.

Preventive maintenance protects returns

Gutter cleaning, drainage management, filters, caulking, inspections, pest control, and small repairs may extend system life and reduce damage.

Deferred maintenance compounds

Deferred maintenance can lower rentability, increase resident complaints, damage adjacent systems, create insurance problems, and reduce sale value.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Calling reserves profit because the money remains in the bank.
  • Using emergency reserves for cosmetic upgrades.
  • Waiting for complete failure before planning replacement.
  • Assuming insurance covers ordinary wear and deferred maintenance.
📝 Try It Yourself
  • List five major systems in a sample property.
  • Estimate remaining life and replacement cost.
  • Create a monthly reserve target for one system.
  • Write a preventive-maintenance task that could extend useful life.
