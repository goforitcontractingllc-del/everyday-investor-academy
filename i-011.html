House Hacking

House hacking uses part of an owner-occupied property to produce income or reduce the owner's housing cost.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: House hacking can lower the cost of living and create early landlord experience, but the owner must understand financing, occupancy rules, fair housing, leases, privacy, repairs, taxes, and shared-space boundaries.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

Common versions include living in one unit of a duplex, renting bedrooms, using a permitted ADU, or renting another portion of the property.

Owner-occupied financing may have different down-payment and eligibility rules than investment-property financing.

The rental income can reduce the owner's out-of-pocket housing cost, but it also creates management and legal responsibilities.

Clickable terms: House Hacking, Owner Occupancy, Housing Offset

🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Owner occupancy

The borrower lives in the property as a primary residence.

Rental unit

The separately rentable space or room.

Shared housing

Residents share some interior or exterior areas.

Housing offset

Rental income reducing the owner's housing expense.

Lease

The written agreement governing the rental relationship.

Occupancy rule

A lender, zoning, code, or program requirement about who may live there.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Assuming every room or structure can legally be rented.
  • Using rent projections that ignore vacancy and shared utilities.
  • Failing to disclose the intended occupancy to the lender.
  • Using informal verbal agreements.
  • Ignoring fair-housing and habitability duties.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. Choose the exact house-hack model.
  2. Verify zoning, permits, occupancy, and lender rules.
  3. Create a complete monthly budget.
  4. Use written screening and lease systems.
  5. Maintain separate rental records and reserves.
🧪 Knowledge Check

1. Does house hacking normally involve owner occupancy?

2. Can every extra room automatically be rented legally?

3. Should rent be treated as guaranteed income?
