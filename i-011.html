House Hacking

House hacking combines personal housing with income from another unit, room, or approved living space.

18–30 minutes Beginner friendly Detailed lesson Atlas terms included
Why this matters: House hacking can reduce housing cost and provide a lower-barrier entry into real estate, but it also mixes home life with resident management and property operations.
📖 The Lesson

Common house-hacking formats

Small multifamily

Live in one unit of a duplex, triplex, or fourplex and rent the others.

Room rental

Rent one or more rooms inside the home.

Accessory dwelling

Rent an approved ADU or live in the ADU while renting the main home.

Temporary strategy

Occupy the property for a period, then convert it to a full rental when lawful and financially workable.

Why owner occupancy matters

Some financing programs distinguish between owner-occupied and investment property. The buyer must accurately follow occupancy representations and loan terms.

Privacy and boundaries

House hacking can mean sharing parking, yards, utilities, walls, laundry, entrances, or even kitchens. Clear agreements and realistic boundaries matter.

Legal spaces only

A basement or garage is not automatically a legal dwelling or bedroom. Verify permits, egress, sanitation, utilities, and occupancy requirements.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Assuming every extra space can legally be rented.
  • Ignoring the emotional cost of living near residents.
  • Using gross rent to justify an unaffordable purchase.
  • Failing to document shared utilities, parking, and access.
📝 Try It Yourself
  • Choose a house-hacking format.
  • List every shared space or system.
  • Estimate owner cost during vacancy.
  • Verify what records would prove the second unit is legal.
