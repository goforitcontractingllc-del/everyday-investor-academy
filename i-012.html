BRRRR and Refinance Risk

BRRRR stands for Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat. The strategy attempts to improve a property, stabilize income, refinance based on the new value, and recycle part of the original cash.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: The refinance is not guaranteed. Appraisal, seasoning, loan-to-value, income, credit, rates, property condition, and lender rules can leave more cash trapped in the deal than expected.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

The purchase must leave enough room for rehab, holding costs, financing, and a conservative after-repair value.

The property must be repaired and rented well enough to support the refinance.

The investor needs a backup plan if the appraisal is low, the loan is delayed, or the new payment is too high.

Clickable terms: BRRRR, Seasoning, Trapped Capital
⚙️ How the System Works
Buy correctlyAcquire at a basis that supports the full plan.
Rehab carefullyControl scope, permits, budget, and schedule.
Rent and stabilizeCreate documented income and operating history.
Refinance conservativelyUse realistic value, loan terms, and debt coverage.
🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Acquisition basis

Purchase price plus qualifying acquisition costs.

Rehab budget

Labor, materials, permits, holding costs, and contingency.

ARV

Estimated value after the planned work.

Seasoning

A lender's required ownership or stabilization period before refinance.

Cash-out refinance

A new loan that may return some owner equity as cash.

Trapped capital

Cash that remains invested after refinancing.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Treating ARV as guaranteed.
  • Underestimating holding and financing costs.
  • Assuming the refinance will return every dollar.
  • Ignoring debt-service coverage after the new loan.
  • Having no plan if refinancing fails.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. Calculate the full acquisition and rehab basis.
  2. Use conservative comparable sales for ARV.
  3. Confirm refinance rules before buying.
  4. Model a lower appraisal and higher interest rate.
  5. Write backup hold, sale, or capital plans.
🧪 Knowledge Check

1. Is the refinance guaranteed?

2. Should holding costs be included?

3. Can capital remain trapped after refinancing?
