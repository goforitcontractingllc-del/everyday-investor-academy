Flipping Houses: When It Works—and When It Does Not

A house flip buys property with the intention of improving and reselling it within a relatively short period.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: Flipping concentrates construction, financing, holding, permit, appraisal, market, and resale risk into one project. A strong resale price does not guarantee profit.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

Profit equals sale proceeds minus acquisition, construction, financing, holding, selling, tax, and unexpected costs.

The project needs a clear scope, realistic schedule, verified contractors, permits, contingency, and conservative resale assumptions.

The investor should know the maximum purchase price before becoming emotionally attached to the property.

Clickable terms: House Flip, Holding Cost, Net Profit
⚙️ How the System Works
Analyze before offeringBuild scope, budget, ARV, timeline, and maximum price.
Control constructionUse contracts, permits, draws, inspections, and change orders.
Prepare the saleFinish punch list, disclosures, staging, and pricing.
Reconcile the projectCalculate actual profit after all costs and taxes.
🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Acquisition cost

Purchase price and closing costs.

Rehab cost

Labor, materials, permits, design, and contingency.

Holding cost

Interest, taxes, insurance, utilities, security, and maintenance during ownership.

Selling cost

Broker, title, escrow, concessions, staging, and transfer-related costs.

ARV

Estimated market value after completed work.

Net profit

Sale proceeds remaining after every project cost.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Using sale price minus purchase price as profit.
  • Ignoring financing and selling costs.
  • Starting without permits or detailed scope.
  • Using one optimistic ARV.
  • Assuming the market will remain unchanged during construction.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. Build a full project budget.
  2. Verify ARV with adjusted comparable sales.
  3. Get written contractor scopes and timelines.
  4. Use a meaningful contingency.
  5. Set a maximum purchase price and walk-away rule.
🧪 Knowledge Check

1. Is sale price minus purchase price the true flip profit?

2. Should selling costs be included?

3. Can market conditions change during the project?
