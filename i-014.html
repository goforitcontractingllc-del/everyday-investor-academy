Small Multifamily Investing

Small multifamily property usually means two- to four-unit residential property, although market and lending definitions can vary.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: Multiple units can spread vacancy risk and create more income than a single unit, but they also multiply leases, maintenance, utilities, resident issues, and capital needs.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

A duplex, triplex, or fourplex may qualify for residential financing depending on occupancy and lender rules.

Each unit should be analyzed separately for rent, lease terms, condition, utilities, and turnover risk.

Shared systems—roof, water line, sewer, parking, exterior, and common areas—can create large costs affecting every unit.

Clickable terms: Rent Roll, Unit Mix, Per-Unit Metric
⚙️ How the System Works
Audit each unitLease, rent, deposit, condition, and occupancy.
Review shared systemsUtilities, roof, parking, access, and exterior.
Build combined operationsIncome, vacancy, management, repairs, and reserves.
Verify financingOwner occupancy, appraisal, debt coverage, and cash requirements.
🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Unit mix

The number and type of units in the property.

Rent roll

A list of units, residents, rents, deposits, and lease terms.

Utility allocation

Who pays water, sewer, garbage, power, and shared services.

Common area

Shared hallways, yards, parking, laundry, or storage.

Expense ratio

Operating expenses compared with effective income.

Per-unit metric

Price, income, expense, or repair amount divided by number of units.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Assuming all units are legal.
  • Using one unit's condition for the whole property.
  • Ignoring shared utility and parking problems.
  • Failing to audit deposits and leases.
  • Underestimating management time.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. Verify the legal unit count.
  2. Audit every lease and deposit.
  3. Inspect each unit and all shared systems.
  4. Build a property-wide operating budget.
  5. Confirm financing and insurance for the actual use.
🧪 Knowledge Check

1. Should every unit be reviewed separately?

2. Can shared systems create property-wide costs?

3. Is every existing unit automatically legal?
