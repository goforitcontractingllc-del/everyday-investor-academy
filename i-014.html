Small Multifamily Investing

Duplexes, triplexes, and fourplexes combine several income streams with shared systems and concentrated management.

18–30 minutesDetailed lessonAtlas terms included
Why this matters: Several units can spread vacancy risk, but they also multiply leases, repairs, utilities, resident issues, and legal-unit questions.
📖 The Lesson

Why small multifamily matters

Small multifamily can support owner occupancy, house hacking, multigenerational living, or full rental operations.

Shared systems

Utilities

Separate or shared meters affect expenses, billing, and resident expectations.

Parking and access

Assigned spaces, driveways, mail, trash, and emergency access must work.

Building systems

Roofs, walls, plumbing, heating, and exterior maintenance may serve several units.

Legal unit count

Each dwelling should be verified through permits and occupancy records.

Unit mix

Unit mix affects rent, turnover, household type, demand, and operating cost.

One vacancy is not all vacancy

Multiple units can reduce total-income disruption, but a major roof, sewer, or insurance issue can affect the whole property at once.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Assuming advertised unit count is legal.
  • Ignoring shared utilities and common-area costs.
  • Treating four units as four independent houses.
  • Underestimating management and turnover volume.
📝 Try It Yourself
  • Verify legal unit count for a sample property.
  • List every shared system.
  • Build a four-unit rent roll.
  • Test cash flow with one unit vacant.
