Commercial Real Estate Basics

Commercial property is valued and operated around business use, lease structure, income durability, and tenant credit.

18–30 minutesDetailed lessonAtlas terms included
Why this matters: Commercial real estate can produce longer leases and larger income streams, but vacancy, financing, environmental, and build-out risks can be substantial.
📖 The Lesson

Major commercial types

Retail

Stores, restaurants, and service businesses.

Office

Professional, medical, and administrative space.

Industrial

Warehouses, shops, distribution, and light manufacturing.

Mixed-use

Commercial and residential uses in one property.

Lease structure

Commercial leases may allocate taxes, insurance, maintenance, and common-area costs differently. A triple-net lease shifts certain expenses to the tenant, but the exact lease controls.

Tenant improvements

Commercial space may require costly build-out. Tenant improvements should be budgeted with permits, accessibility, fire/life safety, and lease term.

Income durability

Investors evaluate tenant business strength, lease length, renewal options, location, alternate uses, and re-leasing cost.

Long leases can be valuable, but replacing a tenant can be expensive.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Assuming commercial leases are standardized.
  • Ignoring environmental review and permitted use.
  • Underestimating vacancy and build-out time.
  • Relying on one specialized tenant with no alternate-use plan.
📝 Try It Yourself
  • Choose one commercial type.
  • List possible tenant improvements.
  • Estimate six months of vacancy and build-out.
  • Identify the permitted-use and accessibility questions.
