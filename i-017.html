Partnerships, LLCs, and Ownership Structures

Ownership structure affects control, liability, taxes, financing, succession, and what happens when people disagree.

18–30 minutesDetailed lessonAtlas terms included
Why this matters: Partnerships often begin with trust and enthusiasm. Problems appear when money, labor, decisions, guarantees, and exits were never written down.
📖 The Lesson

Entity and deal are not the same

An LLC is a state-law entity. Its federal tax classification can vary depending on ownership and elections. The entity does not replace contracts, insurance, good records, or responsible conduct.

What a partnership agreement should address

Contributions

Cash, credit, property, labor, guarantees, and future funding.

Control

Who decides, votes, signs, borrows, leases, and sells.

Economics

Ownership, distributions, fees, tax allocations, and reimbursements.

Exit and conflict

Buyouts, death, disability, default, deadlock, and sale.

Operating agreement

An operating agreement governs internal LLC decisions. Generic templates may miss the exact economics and risk of the property deal.

Personal guarantees

Borrowers may still have personal liability through guarantees, fraud, misuse of funds, or other obligations even when an entity owns the property.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Forming an LLC without an operating agreement.
  • Using equal ownership when contributions and risk are not equal.
  • Failing to document related-party payments.
  • Ignoring lender consent, transfer restrictions, and tax advice.
📝 Try It Yourself
  • List every partner contribution.
  • Assign decision authority.
  • Write a deadlock and exit process.
  • Identify which professionals should review the structure.
