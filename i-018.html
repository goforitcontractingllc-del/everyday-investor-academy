Exit Strategies: Sell, Hold, Refinance, or Exchange

An exit strategy explains how an investor may end, change, or recover capital from an investment.

18–30 minutesDetailed lessonAtlas terms included
Why this matters: Buying is easier when you know what must be true to sell, hold, refinance, transfer, or exchange the property later.
📖 The Lesson

Main exit paths

Sell

Convert the property to cash, subject to market conditions, debt payoff, transaction cost, and taxes.

Hold

Continue operating for income, appreciation, or future options.

Refinance

Replace debt, change terms, or access equity, subject to underwriting and property performance.

Like-kind exchange

A properly structured Section 1031 exchange may defer recognition of gain when qualifying investment or business real property is exchanged for qualifying real property.

Exit price is not net proceeds

Broker fees, closing costs, concessions, loan payoff, repairs, taxes, and other obligations reduce cash received.

Hold decision

Holding is an active choice. Compare future return, capital needs, management burden, tax position, market risk, and alternative uses of the equity.

Exchange complexity

A 1031 exchange has strict legal and tax requirements. It is not simply selling and buying another property later. Use qualified legal and tax professionals before the sale closes.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Waiting until a crisis to choose an exit.
  • Calling sale price profit.
  • Assuming refinance or exchange eligibility is automatic.
  • Ignoring the management burden and future capital needs of holding.
