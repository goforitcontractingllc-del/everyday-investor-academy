Taxes and Depreciation Basics

Rental-property taxes involve income, expenses, depreciation, loss limitations, sale reporting, and records that must be handled carefully.

18–30 minutesDetailed lessonAtlas terms included
Why this matters: Tax benefits are often advertised as if they are automatic. In reality, eligibility, timing, basis, personal use, passive-loss rules, and sale consequences all matter.
📖 The Lesson

Rental income and expenses

Rental income is generally reported, and qualifying expenses may be deductible under current tax rules. Repairs, improvements, travel, interest, taxes, insurance, and management can receive different treatment.

Depreciation

Depreciation is a tax method that allocates the cost of qualifying property over a recovery period. Land itself is not depreciated. Residential rental buildings are generally depreciated under current federal rules using a 27.5-year recovery period, but basis, placed-in-service date, improvements, and personal use matter.

Basis

Adjusted basis begins with acquisition cost and changes through capital improvements, depreciation, and other adjustments.

Loss limitations

Rental losses may be limited by passive activity and at-risk rules. A paper loss does not automatically reduce every kind of income.

Educational only: Tax law is highly fact-specific. Use current IRS guidance and a qualified tax professional.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Depreciating the land.
  • Using purchase price without allocating land and building.
  • Assuming tax loss equals cash loss.
  • Ignoring depreciation when planning a sale.
📝 Try It Yourself
  • Find the current IRS rental-property publication.
  • Separate land from building in a sample purchase.
  • List repairs separately from capital improvements.
  • Write questions for a tax professional before buying.
