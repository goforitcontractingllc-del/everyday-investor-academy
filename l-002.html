Property Boundaries and Legal Descriptions

A property boundary is a legal line, not simply a fence, driveway edge, tree row, or what the seller believes.

45–70 minutesMultiple micro-lessonsDeveloper thinkingScenario basedAtlas connected
Educational notice: This lesson teaches research and decision-making. It is not a survey, title opinion, engineering report, environmental determination, planning approval, or legal advice.
Why this matters: Boundary mistakes can block construction, create neighbor disputes, interfere with financing, and reduce usable land.
Micro-lessonsField scenarioDeveloper assignmentKnowledge check

2.1 — Legal Descriptions

A legal description identifies land in a deed or other recorded instrument.

Common systems include lot-and-block descriptions, metes and bounds, and government survey descriptions.

A street address is useful for location but is not a complete legal description.

2.2 — Surveys and Monuments

A licensed surveyor researches records, measures the site, and interprets boundary evidence.

Corner monuments or pins help mark boundaries, but missing or disturbed markers require professional review.

An old survey may not answer a new question if parcels, roads, easements, or monuments have changed.

2.3 — Fences and Encroachments

A fence may sit on, inside, or outside the legal boundary.

An encroachment occurs when a structure or use crosses a property line or easement area.

Long-standing use does not automatically make the location correct.

2.4 — Boundary Disputes

Boundary disagreements should be handled through records, survey evidence, title review, and qualified legal advice.

Moving or destroying a monument can create serious problems.

Do not rely on a contractor or neighbor to determine a legal boundary.

🧩 The Terms You Must Recognize

Legal description

Recorded words identifying the land.

Lot and block

A description tied to a recorded subdivision plat.

Metes and bounds

A description using directions, distances, and reference points.

Survey monument

A physical marker used to help identify a boundary.

Encroachment

A structure or use crossing a legal line.

Boundary survey

A licensed surveyor’s work locating property boundaries.

🗺️ Real Parcel Scenario

The seller says the fence marks the property line. A GIS image suggests the fence may be six feet inside the parcel, and a detached garage appears close to the other boundary.

Open the developer walkthrough
  1. Treat GIS as a research tool, not a final boundary determination.
  2. Obtain the deed, legal description, plats, and prior surveys.
  3. Hire a licensed surveyor when the location matters to the project.
  4. Review potential encroachments with title and legal professionals.
  5. Do not design construction from an assumed fence line.
