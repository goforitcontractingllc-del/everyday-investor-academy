Comprehensive Plans, Overlays, and Development Capacity

The comprehensive plan describes a community’s long-term land-use direction. Zoning implements that direction, while overlays and special districts add another layer of rules.

Why this matters: A project can match the base zoning and still be restricted by floodplain, wildfire, airport, historic, riparian, hillside, or infrastructure overlays.

7.1 — Comprehensive Plans

A comprehensive plan guides growth, housing, transportation, public facilities, natural resources, and future land use.

Plan designations and zoning can be related but are not always identical.

Changing the plan is usually more difficult than obtaining an ordinary development permit.

7.2 — Overlay Zones

Overlays add requirements to the base zoning.

Common overlays address flood hazards, wildfire, steep slopes, airport safety, historic resources, waterways, or design.

Several overlays may apply to one parcel at the same time.

7.3 — Net Developable Area

Gross acreage is the entire site.

Net developable area removes land needed for roads, easements, utilities, environmental protection, steep slopes, or other constraints.

Density should be tested against the code’s actual calculation method.

7.4 — Future Infrastructure and Planning

Transportation plans, sewer master plans, urban-growth boundaries, and capital-improvement plans can affect timing and cost.

A parcel may be planned for growth but not ready for near-term service.

Future value depends on approvals, infrastructure, market demand, and carrying capacity.

🧩 The Terms You Must Recognize

Comprehensive plan

The long-range policy framework for community land use.

Plan designation

The planned future land-use category for a property.

Overlay zone

Additional mapped standards layered over base zoning.

Gross acreage

The total site area before deductions.

Net developable area

The portion realistically available for development.

Urban growth boundary

A planning boundary controlling where urban development expands.

🗺️ Real Parcel Scenario

A two-acre site has multifamily zoning, but half the parcel falls within a floodplain overlay and a future road corridor crosses the frontage.

Open the developer walkthrough
  1. Map every overlay and planned facility.
  2. Determine how the code calculates net density.
  3. Ask whether the future road requires dedication or setback.
  4. Price floodplain studies, grading, drainage, and access impacts.
  5. Recalculate the project using only verified developable area.
