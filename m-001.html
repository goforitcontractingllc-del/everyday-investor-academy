Your Money Snapshot

A money snapshot is a one-page picture of what comes in, what must go out, what is owed, what is saved, and what needs attention first.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: Money feels impossible when every number lives in a different app, envelope, message, or memory. A snapshot turns the stress into a visible starting point.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

The snapshot is not a budget yet. It is a fact sheet.

Use current numbers and label anything that is only an estimate.

The purpose is not to judge yourself. The purpose is to know what is true enough to choose the next step.

Clickable terms: Cash Flow, Net Worth, Liability
⚙️ How the System Works
CollectGather statements, bills, pay records, and account balances.
SortSeparate income, required costs, debts, and savings.
Mark uncertaintyLabel estimates and missing information.
Choose priorityIdentify what must be handled first.
🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Income

Take-home pay, benefits, support, side income, and irregular income.

Required expenses

Housing, utilities, food, transportation, insurance, care, and taxes.

Debts

Balances, minimums, due dates, interest rates, and legal status.

Savings

Cash, emergency funds, sinking funds, and other available reserves.

Assets

Accounts, vehicles, business property, and other items with value.

Urgent risks

Late rent, shutoff, court, insurance lapse, tax, or safety issues.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Using gross income instead of take-home income.
  • Leaving out annual or irregular expenses.
  • Counting available credit as savings.
  • Avoiding numbers because they feel embarrassing.
  • Trying to solve every problem before completing the snapshot.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. Write every income source and average take-home amount.
  2. List required monthly costs.
  3. List debts with balance, minimum, and status.
  4. Write available cash and savings.
  5. Circle the three most urgent items.
