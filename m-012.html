Understanding Taxes

Taxes are required payments connected to income, payroll, purchases, property, business activity, and other events. The system becomes easier when records, deadlines, and payment responsibilities are separated.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: Tax problems grow when records are missing, returns are not filed, withholding is too low, or self-employment income is spent before tax money is set aside.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

Employees often have income and payroll taxes withheld from pay.

Self-employed people may need to track business income and expenses, pay self-employment tax, and make estimated tax payments.

Filing a return and paying the full balance are different issues. A person may still need to file even when they cannot pay everything at once.

⚙️ How the System Works
Collect recordsIncome forms, expenses, prior returns, and notices.

Calculate responsibilityWithholding, estimated payments, filing, and balance due.
File and address paymentSubmit accurate returns and use official payment options.
🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Federal income tax

Tax on taxable income under federal law.

State income tax

Income tax imposed by a state when applicable.

Payroll tax

Taxes connected to Social Security and Medicare.

Withholding

Tax taken from pay and sent to the government.

Estimated tax

Periodic tax payments made when enough tax is not withheld.

Tax return

The filed document reporting income, deductions, credits, and tax.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Treating gross business deposits as personal spending money.
  • Not filing because the full tax cannot be paid.
  • Mixing personal and business expenses.
  • Ignoring IRS or state notices.
  • Relying on social media for tax rules.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. Create one folder for every tax year.
  2. Collect all income forms and business records.
  3. Separate personal and business activity.
  4. Check withholding or estimated-tax needs.
  5. Use a qualified tax professional when returns are missing or complex.
