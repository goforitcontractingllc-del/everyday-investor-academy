Insurance Basics

Insurance transfers defined financial risks to an insurer in exchange for premiums and compliance with the policy terms.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: Insurance does not make bad events impossible. It limits the financial damage when a covered event happens.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

A premium is the price paid for coverage. A deductible is the amount the insured may need to pay before covered benefits begin.

Coverage limits cap how much the insurer pays, while exclusions identify losses the policy does not cover.

Insurance needs change with housing, vehicles, dependents, health, business activity, property ownership, and income.

🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Premium

The price paid for insurance coverage.

Deductible

The amount the insured pays before certain coverage applies.

Coverage limit

The maximum amount the insurer may pay.

Exclusion

A loss or situation the policy does not cover.

Liability coverage

Protection against certain claims that the insured caused harm.

Claim

A formal request for payment under a policy.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Choosing only by the lowest premium.
  • Assuming every loss is covered.
  • Keeping outdated property or driver information.
  • Using personal insurance for undisclosed business activity.
  • Failing to document property before a loss.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. List the financial risks that could seriously damage the household.
  2. Review current policies, limits, deductibles, and exclusions.
  3. Confirm business or rental activity is properly disclosed.
  4. Create a photo and document inventory.
  5. Review coverage after major life or property changes.
