Debt Payoff Strategies

A debt payoff strategy decides where extra money goes while required payments and essential household costs remain protected.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: The fastest-looking payoff plan can fail if it causes rent, utilities, food, transportation, insurance, or another minimum payment to become late.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

The avalanche method targets the highest interest rate first.

The snowball method targets the smallest balance first.

Hardship plans, negotiated arrangements, or legal advice may be more appropriate when minimums are not affordable.

Clickable terms: Debt Avalanche, Debt Snowball, Hardship Plan
⚙️ How the System Works
Protect essentialsHousing, food, utilities, transport, insurance, safety.
List debtsBalance, rate, minimum, status, and legal risk.
Choose the methodAvalanche, snowball, hardship, or another plan.
Roll forwardMove the freed payment to the next target.
🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Minimum payment

The required payment for the current cycle.

Extra payment

Money paid above the required amount.

Avalanche

Highest interest rate receives extra money first.

Snowball

Smallest balance receives extra money first.

Hardship plan

A temporary creditor-approved modification.

Settlement

An agreement to resolve a debt for an agreed amount.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Paying extra while another debt becomes late.
  • Using all savings with no emergency buffer.
  • Trusting settlement promises without written terms.
  • Ignoring taxes or legal consequences.
  • Choosing a plan that cannot survive variable income.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. List every debt and current status.
  2. Protect essential bills and minimums first.
  3. Choose one target account.
  4. Set a realistic extra-payment amount.
  5. Review the plan monthly.
