Major Purchases

A major purchase is a decision with consequences beyond the sticker price. Vehicles, homes, equipment, education, and large household items create ongoing costs and opportunity costs.

25–35 minutesBeginner friendlyPlain EnglishDetailed lessonAtlas connected
Why this matters: People often compare only the monthly payment and overlook insurance, maintenance, taxes, fees, repairs, financing, and the savings that will no longer be available.
Just tell me simplyHow it worksWhat to do nowKnowledge check
🧠 Just Tell Me Simply

Total cost of ownership includes the purchase price plus ongoing and exit costs.

Cash, financing, leasing, and delaying the purchase each create different tradeoffs.

Affordability means the purchase fits the household after essentials, reserves, and realistic future costs.

Clickable terms: Total Cost of Ownership, Opportunity Cost, Depreciation
⚙️ How the System Works
Define the needProblem the purchase must solve.
Calculate total costPrice, financing, insurance, fees, upkeep, and exit.
Compare alternativesUsed, smaller, delayed, rented, repaired, or financed differently.
Protect the planKeep reserves and avoid hidden obligations.
🧩 The Parts You Need to Recognize

Sticker price

The advertised purchase price.

Total cost of ownership

Purchase plus operating, financing, maintenance, and exit costs.

Down payment

Cash paid toward the purchase.

Opportunity cost

The benefit given up by using money for this purchase.

Depreciation

Loss of value over time.

Exit cost

The cost or loss connected to selling, replacing, or ending the purchase.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Shopping only by monthly payment.
  • Using every dollar of savings for the down payment.
  • Ignoring insurance before buying.
  • Financing optional add-ons without noticing.
  • Treating approval as proof of affordability.
✅ Just Tell Me What to Do

Your action steps

  1. Write the exact need the purchase solves.
  2. Calculate upfront and monthly total cost.
  3. Compare at least three alternatives.
  4. Check the impact on emergency savings and goals.
  5. Wait 24 hours before signing when practical.
🧪 Knowledge Check

1. Is the monthly payment the entire cost?

2. Should insurance be checked before purchase?

3. Does approval prove affordability?
