What an Oregon Property Manager Actually Does

Property management is the licensed work of operating rental real estate for another person while protecting the owner’s asset, handling money correctly, serving residents lawfully, and documenting important decisions.

45–70 minutesScenario basedExam preparationOregon focused
Non-accredited supplemental course: This does not replace Oregon’s approved 60-hour pre-license course, create PSI exam eligibility, provide legal advice, or authorize unlicensed activity.
Why this matters: The job is much bigger than collecting rent. A manager coordinates leasing, resident communication, maintenance, trust money, vendors, owner reporting, compliance, and emergencies.
🧠 Explain It Like I’m New

A property manager acts for the owner under written authority.

In Oregon, licensed property managers manage rental real estate and must maintain at least one clients’ trust account.

Every important action should connect to authority, law, safety, money, or documentation.

Clickable terms: Property Manager, Clients’ Trust Account, Fiduciary Duty

🧩 What You Must Recognize

Owner relationship

Follow lawful written authority and keep the owner informed.

Resident relationship

Administer the lease fairly and professionally.

Operations

Leasing, inspections, notices, maintenance, and vendors.

Money

Rent, deposits, bills, ledgers, trust accounts, and reporting.

Compliance

Oregon real estate law, landlord-tenant law, and fair housing.

Risk control

Documentation, insurance, escalation, and emergency response.

🏢 Field Scenario

At 8:05 AM a resident reports water dripping through the ceiling. At 8:20 AM another resident says water is reaching a light fixture.

Open the professional walkthrough
  1. Treat water near electricity as an urgent safety issue.
  2. Tell residents to avoid the area and follow emergency procedures.
  3. Dispatch qualified help within written authority.
  4. Document times, statements, photos, calls, and owner updates.
  5. Do not delay emergency mitigation merely to obtain the cheapest bid.
🧪 Exam-Style Check

1. Is property management only rent collection?

2. Does written authority matter?

3. Must client money be handled separately?

4. Is documentation part of risk control?
