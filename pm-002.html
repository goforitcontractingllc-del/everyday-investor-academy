The Management Agreement: Your Operating Authority

The management agreement explains what the manager may do, what the owner must do, how money is handled, how fees are earned, and how the relationship ends.

45–70 minutesScenario basedExam preparationOregon focused
Non-accredited supplemental course: This does not replace Oregon’s approved 60-hour pre-license course, create PSI exam eligibility, provide legal advice, or authorize unlicensed activity.
Why this matters: A manager who acts outside the agreement can create owner disputes, financial loss, insurance problems, and regulatory risk.
🧠 Explain It Like I’m New

Read the agreement as an operating map, not just a contract stored after signing.

It should define services, spending authority, fees, reserves, reporting, insurance duties, and termination.

No contract can authorize unlawful conduct.

Clickable terms: Management Agreement, Scope of Authority, Owner Reserve

🧩 What You Must Recognize

Scope of services

The work the manager agrees to perform.

Spending authority

The amount and circumstances in which expenses may be approved.

Management fee

How and when compensation is earned.

Owner reserve

Funds maintained for property expenses.

Insurance duty

Who must obtain and maintain coverage.

Termination clause

How funds, records, keys, and authority are transferred.

🏢 Field Scenario

A roofer says a $7,000 repair is needed today. Routine authority is capped at $750, but emergency authority permits necessary action to prevent more damage.

Open the professional walkthrough
  1. Verify urgency and scope.
  2. Use only authority actually granted.
  3. Approve immediate mitigation if authorized.
  4. Seek owner approval for broader replacement work when time allows.
  5. Save photos, bids, approvals, invoices, and completion records.
🧪 Exam-Style Check

1. Does the agreement define authority?

2. Can it authorize illegal discrimination?

3. Should emergency authority be clear?

4. Should termination address records and funds?
