Marketing a Rental

Create accurate, fair, useful advertising and a consistent inquiry-to-showing process.

20–30 minute readEvergreen coreReviewed July 2026

Start here

Rental marketing is the full process of preparing the property, describing it accurately, communicating terms, responding consistently, and creating a clear path from inquiry to application.

Marketing starts before the listing

Before advertising, the manager should know what is included, what remains under repair, when the property can realistically be shown, who pays each utility, what parking or storage is assigned, what restrictions apply, and whether the home is safe and ready for occupancy.

Advertising too early creates inconsistent answers, missed dates, rushed repairs, and promises that later have to be withdrawn.

Quick take: Describe the property and the process—not the type of person you hope will live there.

What a useful listing should answer

  • Rent and recurring charges.
  • Deposit or how it will be determined.
  • Bedrooms, bathrooms, and approximate size when supportable.
  • Appliances, parking, storage, laundry, yard, and shared spaces.
  • Utilities included or resident-paid.
  • Pet policy and application steps.
  • Availability date and showing process.
  • How to request an accommodation.

Fair, factual wording

Statements such as “perfect for young professionals,” “ideal Christian family,” or “no kids” describe a preferred resident. Stronger wording describes measurable features: second-floor walk-up, two assigned parking spaces, bus access, fenced yard, quiet hours under the lease, or step-free rear entry.

Photos and records

Use current photos

Show every room, exterior, parking, storage, laundry, yard, and shared area.

Show limitations

Stairs, narrow access, shared areas, unusual parking, or unfinished work should not be hidden.

Protect privacy

Do not publish prior-resident documents, medication, family photos, or identifying details.

Archive the listing

Save text, photos, dates, pricing, and later changes.

Inquiry and showing consistency

Similar inquiries should receive similar information. Use a simple script for availability, criteria, showings, application timing, documents, and accommodations. At showings, do not discuss prior residents or give personal opinions about neighborhood safety or who would “fit.”

Pricing and vacancy

Compare similar rentals, but also consider condition, utilities, parking, season, vacancy cost, location, included services, and owner goals. A high price may lengthen vacancy; a low price may reduce income and be hard to correct quickly.

Common mistakes

  • Advertising before rent-ready completion.
  • Preference-based language.
  • Missing fees or utility details.
  • Different answers for different applicants.
  • Old photos or unrealistic move-in dates.
  • Changing terms without documentation.

Try it yourself

Write a two-bedroom listing without describing the ideal resident. Include price, utilities, parking, accessibility details, pet policy, application process, and showing instructions.

Oregon update note

Current through July 2026: Oregon application and screening provisions are in ORS Chapter 90. The 2025 ORS edition does not yet reflect every 2026-session change, so current legislation and official guidance should be checked before using forms or charging screening fees.
