PM-008 · Property Management

Leasing

Turn an approved applicant into a clear, documented tenancy with the right agreement, disclosures, money, and expectations.

20–30 minute readEvergreen coreReviewed July 2026

Start here

Leasing is where marketing promises and approval terms become a written operating agreement for the tenancy.

The lease is more than a form

It identifies who may occupy, what property is included, how much is owed, when payment is due, which utilities belong to whom, what rules apply, and how the agreement ends.

A duplex with shared water, a rural house with a well, and an apartment with assigned parking need different details.

Build the package from approved terms

  • Correct legal names and property address.
  • Term, possession date, rent, fees, and payment methods.
  • Deposits and other lawful charges.
  • Utilities, parking, storage, yard, laundry, and shared areas.
  • Pet or assistance-animal documentation handled correctly.
  • Maintenance, smoking, communication, and emergency rules.
  • Required disclosures and current addenda.

Resolve every promise before signing

If the applicant expects a garage, appliance, repair, concession, or parking space, the writing should confirm it. Replace “owner will fix later” with an exact scope and deadline.

Example: “Owner will replace the missing bedroom blind before possession on August 10, 2026.”

Money needs a clean trail

Every payment should have a receipt, ledger category, deposit record, and explanation of whether it is refundable or nonrefundable where permitted. Money held for one purpose should not be casually moved to another.

Lease orientation

  • How to pay rent.
  • How to request repairs.
  • What qualifies as an emergency.
  • Entry and inspection process.
  • Parking, guests, utilities, and shared spaces.
  • Condition reporting.
  • How and when management responds.

Electronic signatures still need control

Confirm every party received the same version, all attachments are included, dates and fields are correct, and a complete signed copy is delivered.

Common mistakes

  • Outdated forms.
  • Blank fields.
  • Lease terms that do not match advertising.
  • Unclear payment accounting.
  • Verbal side agreements.
  • Missing disclosures or copies.
  • Pet language used for assistance animals.

Practice exercise

For a duplex with shared water, separate electricity, one assigned parking space, and a shared fenced yard, list every clause or addendum needed to define payment, maintenance, access, and use.

Check your understanding

  1. Why build from approved terms?
  2. Why write exact repair promises?
  3. What follows electronic signing?
  4. What belongs in orientation?

Oregon update note

Current through July 2026: Oregon rental agreements operate within ORS Chapter 90 and may be affected by current rules on deposits, fees, disclosures, entry, habitability, notices, and termination. Use current Oregon-specific forms and legal review for unusual clauses.
