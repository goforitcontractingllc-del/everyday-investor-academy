Rent Collection

Use clear systems for payments, ledgers, late rent, communication, and escalation.

20–30 minute readEvergreen coreReviewed July 2026

Start here

Rent collection is the workflow that records what was due, what arrived, when it arrived, how it was applied, what remains, and what lawful step comes next.

The ledger is the source of truth

A resident ledger should show every charge, payment, credit, adjustment, refund, and balance. Before late-rent communication or notices, verify the ledger, portal, bank activity, money orders, returned payments, arrangements, and pending assistance.

A clean workflow

  1. Create lawful charges.
  2. Offer approved payment methods.
  3. Issue or preserve receipts.
  4. Post payments correctly.
  5. Review balances on schedule.
  6. Communicate consistently.
  7. Apply current law before fees or notices.
  8. Document arrangements.
  9. Reconcile funds to the ledger.

Payment methods

Online portal

Trackable, but staff must understand posting dates, partial-payment settings, reversals, and fees.

Check or money order

Needs date-received control, secure handling, deposit record, and receipt.

Cash

Creates audit and security risk and needs strict dual controls if accepted.

Assistance payments

May come from third parties and require correct allocation and documentation.

Partial payments

Before accepting, rejecting, or applying a partial payment, understand current Oregon law, the lease, pending notices, policy, and whether acceptance affects available remedies. Avoid casual promises unless authorized and documented.

Factual communication

State the ledger balance, due date, approved payment methods, known pending payment, and next step. Avoid shame, threats, public contact, or unauthorized disclosure.

Example: “Our ledger shows $850 unpaid as of July 8. Please review the portal and contact management if you believe the balance is incorrect.”

Payment plans

  • Total balance.
  • Amounts and dates.
  • How new rent is handled.
  • Payment application.
  • Missed-payment result.
  • Owner authority.
  • Legal review when needed.

Common mistakes

  • Notice from an inaccurate ledger.
  • Untracked money orders or cash.
  • Inconsistent payment application.
  • Partial payments accepted without review.
  • Unauthorized plans.
  • Privacy violations.
  • Emotional or threatening language.

Numbers example

A resident owes $1,500 rent and $75 in another lawful charge, then pays $1,000. The ledger must show exactly how the payment was applied and the remaining balance. “Paid most of it” is not accounting.

Practice exercise

Create a sample ledger with rent, a payment, a returned payment, a credit, and a partial payment. Then write a factual resident message using only the ledger.

Check your understanding

  1. Why verify the ledger first?
  2. Why can partial payments require review?
  3. What belongs in a payment plan?
  4. Why avoid emotional language?
Open the answer guide

The ledger determines the true balance; partial payments can affect remedies; plans need amounts, dates, application, new rent, default terms, and authority; factual language protects privacy and clarity.

Oregon update note

Current through July 2026: Oregon rules on rent, fees, nonpayment, notices, payment acceptance, and termination are in ORS Chapter 90 and may be affected by recent legislation and local ordinances. The 2025 ORS edition does not yet incorporate every 2026-session change.
