What Are Your Rights?

Learn the difference between a legal right, a responsibility, a contract term, a policy, and a practical remedy.

18–30 minutes Beginner friendly Detailed lesson Atlas terms included
Why this matters: People often know something feels unfair but do not know whether it violates a law, a contract, a policy, or simply good practice.
Important: This lesson teaches general legal literacy. Laws, deadlines, exceptions, and local rules change. Verify the exact rule with an official source or qualified legal professional before acting.
📖 The Lesson

Rights are only one part of the picture

A legal right is a protection or authority recognized by law. A responsibility is something a person is required to do. A contract term is a promise agreed to by the parties. A policy is a rule created by an organization, but a policy cannot override controlling law.

Five questions to ask

  1. What happened?
  2. Which law, contract, or policy may apply?
  3. What evidence exists?
  4. What remedy is available?
  5. What deadline controls the next step?

A right without a remedy can still be hard to enforce

A remedy is what the law allows someone to seek after a violation, such as correction, damages, a complaint, an injunction, mediation, or court relief.

Unfair is not always unlawful

Some conduct is rude, inconsistent, or harmful without fitting a specific legal claim. Legal literacy means separating emotion, facts, evidence, and the actual rule.

Core lesson: Ask “What rule applies, what proof exists, and what action is available?”

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Assuming unfair treatment automatically proves a legal violation.
  • Relying on memory instead of preserving evidence.
  • Waiting until a deadline passes before seeking help.
  • Using social media summaries as the final legal authority.
📝 Try It Yourself
  • Describe one issue using only facts.
  • Separate the possible right, responsibility, and contract term.
  • List the evidence you would preserve.
  • Find the official source that controls the issue.
