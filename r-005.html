Fair Housing and Equal Access

Understand protected classes, discriminatory treatment, reasonable accommodations, retaliation, and complaint pathways.

18–30 minutes Beginner friendly Detailed lesson Atlas terms included
Why this matters: Fair-housing law applies to much more than refusing to rent. Advertising, screening, financing, rules, services, and disability accommodations can all matter.
Important: This lesson teaches general legal literacy. Laws, deadlines, exceptions, and local rules change. Verify the exact rule with an official source or qualified legal professional before acting.
📖 The Lesson

Federal protected classes

The federal Fair Housing Act prohibits housing discrimination based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, and disability.

Oregon adds protections

Oregon housing law includes additional protected categories. Always verify the current list with BOLI because state and local protections can change.

Equal standards

Applicants should be evaluated under consistent, lawful criteria. Different deposits, options, terms, treatment, or steering based on a protected class may violate fair-housing law.

Disability-related requests

A reasonable accommodation is a change to a rule, policy, practice, or service that may be needed for a person with a disability to use and enjoy housing. A reasonable modification concerns a physical change.

Retaliation

Retaliation for asserting fair-housing rights or filing a complaint can itself be unlawful.

⚠️ Common Mistakes
  • Using different standards for different applicants.
  • Writing advertisements that express unlawful preference.
  • Ignoring a disability-related request.
  • Assuming private housing is never covered.
📝 Try It Yourself
  • Review one housing advertisement for preference language.
  • Find the current federal and Oregon protected classes.
  • Write a neutral screening criterion.
  • List the records needed for an accommodation request.
✅ Knowledge Check

1. Can fair-housing rules apply to advertising and financing?

2. Is a reasonable accommodation always a physical alteration?

⭐ Field Note

Consistency is protection: use written criteria, apply them evenly, and document legitimate reasons for decisions.
